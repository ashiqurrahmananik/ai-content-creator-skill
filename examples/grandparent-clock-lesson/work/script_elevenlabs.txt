[warm, amused] My grandfather fixed everything with a butter knife.
[amused] The toaster. The garden gate. The radio that only played one song.
[nostalgic] People left him broken things and came back with working things, and he never once used a proper tool.
[soft] One summer, he put a stopped clock on the workbench and said, "Copy me."
[curious] I was nine. I thought this would be easy.
[deadpan] He tapped the back. I tapped the back.
[amused] He squinted into the gears. I squinted so hard my cheeks hurt.
[playful] He turned the clock upside down. I turned it upside down.
[curious] He frowned at it. I frowned back. He nodded, as if I had said something clever.
[focused] Then he did the strange part. He closed his eyes, held the clock against his ear, and tapped one spot no bigger than a fingernail.
[determined] I copied him. Nothing. He did it again. I did it again. Still nothing.
[stern, gentle] "Again," he said.
[frustrated] "I can't hear anything," I said.
[gentle] "Again," he said.
[quiet] So we did it again. Tap. Listen. Tap. Listen.
[observant] The workshop smelled of dust and warm metal. Outside, the whole street was making summer noise — bikes, sprinklers, somebody's radio.
[hushed] Inside, there was only that small silence between us, and my grandfather's breathing.
[amused] His old friend Pell came in with a broken fan, saw our faces, and stopped in the doorway. "Lesson in progress," she whispered, and backed out again.
[quiet] By the fourth try, my hand knew where to go before my brain did. Tap. Listen. Tap.
[curious] The clock still didn't move. But I had started to hear things I had never noticed: the pipe in the wall, a fly at the window, his sleeve brushing the bench.
[soft wonder] The workshop was full of sounds. I just had to stop making my own.
[resolute] I wanted to say it was stupid. I wanted to say the clock was broken and we should get a screwdriver.
[hesitant] But every time I opened my mouth, he tapped that spot again, so I closed it.
[whispers] Then he leaned close and whispered, "Now close your eyes."
[hushed] I closed them. At first there was nothing at all.
[surprised] Then, from somewhere in the dark, a tiny tick.
[quiet wonder] Not the clock in my hands. Something smaller, further away, steady as a heartbeat.
[surprised] I opened my eyes so fast the bench seemed to jump.
[realization] The clock in my hands was still stopped at four twelve. Still as a stone. The tick was coming from his pocket.
[soft laugh] He was holding a second clock. He had been listening to it the whole time.
[warm, proud] I stared at him. He shrugged, like I was the one who had missed something obvious.
[warm, proud] "You were listening," he said. "That's the whole job."
