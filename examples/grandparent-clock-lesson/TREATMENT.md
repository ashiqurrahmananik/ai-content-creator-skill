# The Whole Job — treatment

- Project: `~/shorts/grandparent-clock-lesson`
- Style: Paper Pop-up Book (`paper-popup`). Cut-paper actors with white borders performing in a warm, lamp-lit pop-up book on a real desk, shot at macro scale.
- Format: 1080×1920 vertical, 30 fps (60 fps favoured by the style, but 2:25 at 60 fps doubles render cost; 30 fps keeps the film inside the user's under-3-minute budget).
- Voice: 145.01 s supplied Kokoro audio, narrator JUN / af_bella, dialogue IVO / bm_george, JUN / af_bella, PELL / bf_emma. English, subtitles burned in plus `subtitles_en.srt`. No storyboard requested.
- Length cost note given to user: a 2:25 film takes substantially more render time than the style's 30–60 s default.

## 1. Three candidate structures

A. **One spread, one pull at a time.** The whole lesson happens on a single workbench spread. Every attempted fix springs a new paper tab; the final pull releases the pocket watch from Ivo's coat. Chosen: the physical repetition reads instantly and the one unusual tab remains legible at thumbnail size.

B. **Countdown of five failed fixes.** Five cards flip past like a tally, best last. Rejected: it turns a warm lesson into a list and spends the reveal on a gadget instead of the relationship.

C. **Two books facing each other.** Jun's book of copied actions and Ivo's book of listening trade the clock across the gutter. Rejected: two spreads divide attention and the 2:25 runtime cannot establish both.

Chosen: **A**, with one page turn at the pivot (about 56 s) when "doing" becomes "listening". The turn is the film's native move; the last card stays standing after everything else folds.

## 2. Logline and arc

Logline: A boy copies every move his grandfather makes while repairing a stopped clock, then discovers the tick he was chasing was in the old man's pocket all along.

Setup (0–35 s): Ivo fixes anything with a butter knife; Jun copies his tapping, squinting and upside-down reasoning on a dead clock. Turn (35–112 s): Ivo stops using his hands and starts listening; Jun copies the motions but hears nothing, and the workshop's real sounds slowly fill the silence. Ending (112–146 s): eyes closed, Jun hears a small tick that is not the clock in his hands — it is Ivo's pocket watch. The lesson was listening, not fixing.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Macro tabletop craft film**: learn the scale mismatch of flat cards under real lamp light, shallow focus and cut-out shadows. Do not take any product, set design or shot.
- **A handmade pop-up book's fold-out spread**: learn back-to-front pop choreography, the page curl as a scene change, and the pull-tab as an event. Do not take any book's characters, artwork, typography or story.
- Original in every visible particular: Ivo, Jun, Pell, the butter knife, the stopped brass clock at four twelve, the hidden pocket watch, the workshop, the final standing card.

## 8. Subtitle and title design

Burned-in English subtitles, bottom centre, cream with thick dark-brown outline and soft shadow, ~40 px at 1080 width, held at least max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). `subtitles_en.srt` exported alongside. Speech bubbles live in the paper world as white cards with an ink outline and a tail projected to the speaker; they pop with overshoot and type on with blips. One title object: the book cover card swings on strings for the last two seconds with "The Whole Job"; no opening title card, so the hook is frame one. No logos, brands, real people or copied artwork.
